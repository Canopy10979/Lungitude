Where the research data comes from

Clinical skin photographs

PAD-UFES-20 contains smartphone skin photographs and lesion labels. Its cancer categories include melanoma, basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma. A research dataset alone does not establish accuracy for your photo.

Read about PAD-UFES-20 ↗

Other skin-image research

ISIC includes standardized skin photography and dermoscopy collections. Dermoscopy uses specialized optics, so results from those images do not automatically transfer to ordinary phone cameras.

Read about ISIC ↗

Breast measurement research

The separate breast model requires all 30 Wisconsin sampled-cell measurements. It does not analyze breast photographs or recordings. A separate trained breast artifact is not included in this restoration; availability depends on the locally installed model. This workflow has no clinical validation.

Open breast measurement research. Skin scores and breast classifications are not fused because they concern different inputs and target conditions.

What the skin workflow actually does

  • Captures an eight-second local recording or a still image.
  • Retains up to three frames ranked by an experimental sharpness heuristic.
  • Sends only a selected still image after separate consent.
  • Shows experimental image-category scores when analysis is available.

It does not automatically verify that skin or a lesion is present. It does not infer growth from an eight-second recording. An image-category score is not your probability of cancer. Skin-tone, lighting, camera and site differences need independent evaluation.

How research results need to be checked

  1. Training and evaluation must use separate patients, with results reported for every lesion category.
  2. Testing must include independent clinical photographs that were not used to develop the models.
  3. Real-phone testing must cover focus, lighting, orientation and interrupted recordings.
  4. Reports must include unsuccessful captures and performance differences across relevant groups.

Clinical concern takes precedence over model output. A new, different, changing, itching or bleeding spot should be discussed with a dermatologist.
