Lung cancer screening, made walkable

Three minutes with your phone.
One walk to a screening that can find lung cancer early.

Caught early, 65% of people with lung cancer live 5 years or more. Caught late, 10% do. A low-dose CT scan finds it early, but in Washington only 15.8% of the people who qualify get one.

  • Your answers go into PLCOm2012, a published risk model used by doctors.
  • Your microphone times one hard exhale. A long exhale can be a sign of blocked airways.
  • Your camera checks your finger shape for clubbing, a sign linked to lung disease.
  • Your location finds places you can walk to, and gives you a note for your doctor.

Lungitude is a screening guide, not a diagnosis. Audio and photos stay on your device. Only numbers (like "7.2 seconds") go to the server.

Step 4 of 6 · Microphone

One hard exhale

  1. Sit up. Hold the phone 20 cm (8 in) from your mouth.
  2. Breathe in as deeply as you can.
  3. When the ring turns green, blow out as hard and as long as you can, mouth wide open.
Ready

AI lung-sound check (experimental)

Press the bottom of your phone (the microphone) flat on your chest, just below the collarbone. Breathe slowly and deeply through your open mouth for 15 seconds. The AI listens for crackles and wheezes. It does not detect cancer.

Doctors call this the forced expiratory time. The audio does not leave your device.

Step 5 of 6 · Camera

Finger and pulse check

A. Side photo of your index finger

Put your finger flat on a table. Take the photo from the side, at table height. Measure 3 times. A new photo for each time is best.

B. The window test

Press the backs of your two ring fingers together, nail to nail. Look for a small diamond-shaped gap of light.

C. Nail-bed color (experimental)

Pale nail beds can be a sign of low hemoglobin. This is the signal that AnemoDx, the WA-01 2025 winner, used. Here it is one small input, not a result. Take a photo of your nails next to white paper. Tap a nail bed, then tap the paper.

D. Pulse (camera and flash)

Cover the back camera and the flash with the tip of your index finger. Press lightly. Keep still for 25 seconds. The camera sees each heartbeat as a small change in red light.

Use your fingertip, not your wrist or arm. The camera must see light pass through the skin, and the fingertip is thin enough for that.
